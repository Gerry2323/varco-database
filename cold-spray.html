Screening estimate—not a qualified spray parameter.

The semi-empirical calculation uses bulk-property data. Powder strength, particle temperature and size, substrate, nozzle, gas, pressure, and morphology can shift the real threshold. Confirm with diagnostics and deposition-efficiency testing.

Applicability: the generalized Schmidt model is intended primarily for plastically deformable metallic feedstocks. Do not treat a result for a pure ceramic, regolith, or brittle standalone powder as evidence that conventional cold-spray bonding will occur; those systems require a validated metal matrix, binder, or a different deposition model.

Model and required inputs

The tool uses the Schmidt-type semi-empirical relationship with default calibration factors F₁ = 1 and F₂ = 0.25:

vcrit = k₁√{4F₁[σ(1 − (Tp−TR)/(Tm−TR))]/ρ + F₂cp(Tm−Tp)}

k₁ = (dp/25 µm)−0.1,   TR = 20 °C

  • ρ: density; σUTS: ultimate tensile strength; cp: specific heat.
  • Tm: melting temperature; Tp: estimated particle temperature immediately before impact.
  • dp: D50 or the midpoint of a reported particle-size range. The 25 µm reference diameter and exponent 0.1 follow the generalized model.
  • Default F₁ = 1 and F₂ = 0.25 are generalized screening values; replace F₁ only when your powder has been experimentally calibrated.
  • Optional process mode compares the adjusted critical threshold with a measured or simulated impact velocity at the entered standoff distance. A substrate factor must come from testing or a validated particle–substrate model; 1.0 means no adjustment.
